**RSI to AGI Is Still Missing a Hybrid Digital and Physical AGI Infra Benchmark**

GLM-5.3-powered Infra Agent built a production-grade inference service from scratch on a cluster of over 100,000 domestic chips, boosting end-to-end throughput to 3x the baseline in under two weeks, and it has already been validated under real traffic. The digital-side RSI loop — "models optimize the system, the system serves the models" — has already started turning.

But that is only half of RSI.

Digital agents can optimize their own inference engines and training frameworks, but they cannot maintain the physical infrastructure that runs these systems. Data centers, servers, cables, power, cooling — all of this still depends entirely on humans. Meta is already testing Kinova Gen3 for server power cycling, ABB four-wheeled platforms for component re-insertion, and Watney Robotics for cable swapping. One Meta employee estimated that a successful cable-swapping system could eventually eliminate up to 80% of the workload for certain roles. But the technology is still far off: off-the-shelf robots struggle with corners and cables, grayscale cameras cannot distinguish green from red indicator lights, and internally Meta admits its robots are not yet capable of the dense cabling required for Nvidia GB300 systems.

Microsoft Research has taken a more systematic approach. In a HotNets paper, they proposed the concept of "self-maintaining systems" — software-controlled robots that automatically perform hardware maintenance and repair. They have already built basic robots that support optical module re-insertion, cleaning, and fiber cable replacement, and they define hardware maintenance as the "lowest layer" of the entire computing stack, requiring upper layers to precisely control maintenance timing and methods through cross-layer communication.

But data center maintenance is only the first layer of physical RSI.

Deeper still: can embodied systems maintain and optimize *themselves*? Work published by Professor Zhu Yanhe from Zhao Jie's team at Harbin Institute of Technology in the *International Journal of Extreme Manufacturing* shows that modular robots achieve autonomous fault tolerance and self-repair through collaborative reconfiguration. When a joint fails, instead of replacing it with a spare module, the robot restores operational capability through morphological change. In preset extreme multi-failure scenarios, compared with methods relying on fixed-configuration redundancy, collaborative reconfiguration raised the fault-tolerance ceiling by 7x. Columbia University demonstrated an even more radical version earlier: robots can autonomously harvest parts from other robots to repair themselves, and even complete "upgrades."

There is a key distinction behind this. The Physical RSI framework recently proposed by MirroS puts it clearly: digital RSI operates in a "world that has already been written" — fixed rules, finite states, clear objectives. But the rules of the physical world have never been fully written down. A robot does not know what will appear in the room. Failure does not necessarily mean it made the wrong choice; it may mean that the world it uses to find answers is itself missing a piece of reality. Therefore, Physical RSI must not only improve the Actor that executes tasks, but also simultaneously expand the World Model on which the Actor relies. When a robot dog gets tangled in a cable, the system must first determine: is the world model failing to understand the relationship between cables, tension, and motion? Or does it understand but not know how to escape? The former is an environment gap, requiring world model expansion; the latter is a skill gap, requiring Actor improvement.

At the deepest layer: can physical agents develop the materials they themselves use? The EAI Scientist from Yu Xuefeng's team at the Shenzhen Institute of Advanced Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences, is an embodied robotic scientist. Its upper body consists of biomimetic dual arms, and its mobile base can autonomously navigate in a laboratory. The team defines it as: "How can robots be made lighter, how can sensors be made — we call this manufacturing embodiment, embodiment manufacturing." Their MARS system uses 19 LLM agents to coordinate 16 domain tools, achieving closed-loop autonomous material discovery. It optimized perovskite nanocrystal synthesis within 10 iterations and designed a biomimetic "core-shell-crown" structured water-stable perovskite composite within 3.5 hours. In the new energy materials direction, they helped a company build a 300,000-ton lithium iron phosphate production line, and the partner company grew by 541% last year.

Putting these pieces together, the logic chain is clear:

Digital agents optimize inference and training → physical agents maintain the data centers running these systems → physical agents maintain and upgrade their own hardware → physical agents develop the new materials they themselves need.

Each layer is "agents improving the infrastructure that agents need to run." This is what true comprehensive self-improvement looks like.

RSI research is advancing rapidly. AI4AI-Bench tests whether agents can rewrite training algorithms, and the strongest system reaches less than one-fifth of the theoretical optimum. RSI-Exam checks whether improvements transfer to sealed datasets — "improvements that cannot transfer do not score." Zetta achieves closed-loop Harness self-evolution in physical execution, reaching 90.8% and 93.6% on LIBERO-Pro and RoboCasa respectively. SHAPER evolves skills and Harness under frozen model parameters, and SkillGate raises a 9B policy's success rate from 40.8% to 53.2%. EmboCoach-Bench lets agents autonomously develop embodied strategies, surpassing human engineering baselines by an average of 26.5% across 32 RL/IL tasks.

But each of these works covers only one dimension of RSI. No one has, within the same long-horizon task, required an agent to simultaneously optimize digital infrastructure and physical operations capability, and tested whether improvements in the two dimensions can mutually reinforce each other through physical-digital coupling.

So we want to take the first step:

Launch a simulated hybrid Digital + Physical long-horizon task benchmark environment, to see how far we really are from "full-stack self-improvement."

The benchmark is called: **RSI for AGI Infra**.

There is still a long way to go. The GLM team itself says the system has not yet reached the stage of fully autonomous design and training of next-generation models. Meta's robots are still far from ready to take over GB300 cabling. Harbin Institute of Technology's self-repair strategies are currently validated in space and deep-sea scenarios, and moving them into data centers will require substantial engineering work. EAI Scientist's "embodiment manufacturing" is still at the basic materials level, several orders of magnitude away from "robots building better robots themselves."

But at least we can put up the target first.

The first half of RSI is making AI a better problem solver. The second half is letting AI continuously expand the world it can understand and transform — including the physical world, including itself. And this second half is currently missing a hybrid Digital and Physical AGI Infra Benchmark.

**RSI to AGI现在还差个混合Digital and Physical的AGI Infra Benchmark**

GLM-5.3 驱动的 Infra Agent，在超过10万张国产芯片的集群上从零搭建生产级推理服务，不到两周把端到端吞吐提升到基线的3倍，已投入真实流量检验。数字侧的RSI循环——“模型优化系统、系统再服务模型”——已经开始转了。

但这只是RSI的一半。

数字Agent能优化自己的推理引擎和训练框架，却没法维护运行这些系统的物理基础设施。机房、服务器、线缆、电源、散热——目前仍然完全依赖人类。Meta已经在测试用 Kinova Gen3 做服务器电源循环、用 ABB 四轮平台做组件重新安插、用 Watney Robotics 做线缆交换。一位 Meta 员工估计，成功的线缆交换系统最终可能消除某些角色高达80%的工作量。但技术还差得远：库存机器人难以处理角落和电缆，灰度相机无法区分红绿指示灯，内部也承认机器人尚不具备 Nvidia GB300 系统所需的密集布线能力。

微软研究院走得更系统。他们在 HotNets 上提出“自维护系统”概念——软件控制机器人自动执行硬件维护和修复，已经做出支持光模块重新安插、清洁和光纤线缆更换的基础机器人，并把硬件维护定义为整个计算栈的“最底层”，要求上层通过跨层通信来精细控制维护时机和方式。

但机房维护只是物理RSI的第一层。

再往深一层，具身系统能不能维护和优化“自己”？哈工大赵杰团队的朱延河教授在《International Journal of Extreme Manufacturing》发表的工作显示：模块化机器人通过协作重构实现自主容错和自修复。关节失效了，不需要备用模块替换，机器人通过形态变化恢复操作能力。在预设的多故障极端场景中，相比依赖固定构型冗余的方法，协作重构把容错上限提升了7倍。哥伦比亚大学更早展示过更激进的版本：机器人可以自主从其他机器人身上拆取部件来修复自己，甚至完成“升级”。

这背后有一个关键区分。MirroS 最近提出的 Physical RSI 框架说得很清楚：数字RSI在一个“已经写好的世界”里运行——规则固定、状态有限、目标明确。但物理世界的规则从来没有被完整写下来。机器人不知道房间里会出现什么，失败不一定意味着做错了选择，也可能意味着它用来找答案的那个世界本身缺了一部分现实。所以 Physical RSI 不只改进执行任务的 Actor，还必须同步扩展 Actor 赖以行动的 World Model。一只机器狗被电缆缠住，系统要先判断：是世界模型没理解电缆、张力和运动之间的关系？还是理解了但不知道如何脱困？前者是 environment gap，要扩展世界模型；后者是 skill gap，要提升 Actor。

再往最深一层，物理Agent能不能研发自己用的材料？中科院深圳先进技术研究院喻学锋团队的 EAI Scientist 是一台具身智能机器人科学家，上半身是仿生双臂，移动底盘能在实验室里自主导航。团队给它的定义是：“机器人如何做得更轻量化，传感器怎么做——我们叫制造具身、具身制造。” 他们的 MARS 系统用19个LLM智能体协调16个领域工具，实现了闭环自主材料发现，在10轮迭代内优化了钙钛矿纳米晶合成，3.5小时内设计出仿生“核-壳-冠”结构的水稳定钙钛矿复合材料。在新能源材料方向，他们帮助企业建立了一条30万吨的磷酸铁锂产线，合作企业去年增长达541%。

把这几件事串起来看，逻辑链条是清楚的：

数字Agent优化推理和训练 → 物理Agent维护运行这些系统的机房 → 物理Agent维护和升级自身硬件 → 物理Agent研发自身所需的新材料。

每一层都是“Agent改进Agent运行所需的基础设施”。这才是真正的全面自迭代。

RSI研究正在快速推进。AI4AI-Bench 测试Agent能否重写训练算法，最强系统只达到理论最优值的不到五分之一。RSI-Exam 检验改进能否迁移到密封数据集，“不能迁移的改进不得分”。Zetta 在物理执行中实现了闭环Harness自进化，LIBERO-Pro和RoboCasa上分别达到90.8%和93.6%。SHAPER 在冻结模型参数下进化技能与Harness，SkillGate把一个9B策略的成功率从40.8%拉到53.2%。EmboCoach-Bench 让Agent自主开发具身策略，在32个RL/IL任务上平均超越人类工程基线26.5%。

但这些工作各自只覆盖了RSI的一个维度。没有人在同一个长程任务里，要求Agent同时优化数字基础设施和物理运维能力，并检验两个维度的改进能否通过物理-数字耦合相互增强。

所以我们想先迈出一步：

推出一个仿真环境下的混合 Digital + Physical 长程任务 benchmark 环境，看看距离“全栈自举”到底有多远。

Benchmark 名字就叫：**RSI for AGI Infra**。

这条路还有很远。GLM 团队自己说，系统尚未达到完全自主设计和训练下一代模型的阶段。Meta 的机器人也还远没有准备好接管 GB300 的布线。哈工大的自修复策略目前验证在太空和深海场景，搬进机房还需要大量工程化工作。EAI Scientist 的“具身制造”还停留在基础材料层面，离“机器人自己造更好的机器人”差着好几个数量级。

但至少可以把靶子先立起来。

RSI 的上半场，是让 AI 成为更好的问题解决者。它的下半场，是让 AI 不断扩展自己能够理解和改造的世界——包括物理世界，包括它自己。而这个下半场，现在缺一个混合 Digital 和 Physical 的 AGI Infra Benchmark。
